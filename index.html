What is this?

A helper that sorts every noise alert, so our team can spend their time where it counts.

Our team keeps the part that needs a person: checking in and making the call. The helper does the rest. It gathers the facts, shares one tidy alert, and writes it all down. Later, when a case is clear, it can send guests a friendly first reminder.

      How it connects

      Build on what already works.

      Voyage already gets NoiseAware alerts and matches them to the booking. Its Slack bot, Alex, posts them for a few homes today. Turn that on for every home that needs extra care, send it all to one place, and let an automation tool like Make.com or Zapier do the rest. No new engineering to start.

      Why not NoiseAware's own auto-reminder?

      We tried it and turned it off. It texted guests every 10 minutes for every alert, even ones caused by wind or a broken sensor. That is not the welcome we want. Our version checks our rules first, and a person signs off before any guest hears from us.

      Why not the NoiseAware API?

      It is still an option for later. It gives the noise graph and which sensor fired, which helps spot bad sensors. But Alex already does the hard part, matching alerts to bookings, so we start there and add the API only if we need it.

      The plan

      Four small steps. Each one earns trust for the next.

      The helper never texts a guest on its own until it makes the same call our team would, 9 times out of 10.

      1. Step 1. No new tools

        Get the data right

        Send alerts for every home that needs extra care to one place. Note each home's camera and neighbors. Fill in the care level for homes missing one.

        Done when: every one of those homes shows up in one place, with its camera and neighbors noted.

      2. Step 2

        Sort and write down

        The helper reads each alert, adds who is staying, the camera and the neighbors, and shares it. A teammate taps what happened. The helper writes it down.

        Done when: no one has to refresh a dashboard, and the helper writes 9 in 10 reports.

      3. Step 3

        Find faulty sensors

        Notice alerts from empty homes and sensors acting strangely. Draft reminder texts for the team to send.

        Done when: a faulty sensor list is reviewed weekly and each one has a repair owner.

      4. Step 4

        A friendly first reminder

        On clear cases, the helper sends guests a kind first note. Before that, it practices for two weeks, only showing the team what it would have sent.

        Done when: it makes the same call as the team 9 times out of 10.

      The details

      Requirements, in plain English.

      Who it is for
      Our monitoring team on every shift, their lead, and the area managers they call for backup.
      Goals
      No one refreshes a dashboard. Every alert arrives with the facts attached. Every incident is written down once, by the helper. Faulty sensors get found and fixed. Guests only hear from us when it helps.
      Not goals
      Replacing people. Judging noise from camera audio. Calling police or the city, or asking guests to leave.
      How we will know it works
      How fast we respond. How many alerts the helper sorts on its own. How many write-ups it handles. How many "just in case" texts we send. How many faulty sensors are waiting for repair.
      The rules it follows
      From our noise playbook. Noise counts after 5 minutes over the limit. Each stay gets the higher of the home's and the guest's care level. Most homes get some room unless neighbors have complained lately. Homes that need extra care get a camera check first. For the most sensitive homes, we text and call right away. After a reminder, we wait 10 minutes before calling for backup.

      Always a person

      • Listening in and judging the noise
      • Calls with the city or police
      • Second reminders, and asking guests to leave
      • Neighbor and owner complaints

      Risks and how we handle them

      • Wrong home data. Some homes share an ID in the source export. We check IDs before the helper uses them.
      • Missing care levels. About 1 in 7 homes has none yet. We fill them in during step 1.
      • Rules are out of date. Our playbook is from 2023. The team confirms noise limits by market before step 2.
      • Texting guests when we shouldn't. No automatic texts until the helper matches the team 9 times out of 10.
      • Shared logins. Passwords move out of documents and get rotated.

      What's next

      What we need to start.

      • Product confirms who can turn on Alex noise alerts for every home that needs extra care, in one place.
      • The monitoring team notes the camera account, camera name and neighbors for each of those homes.
      • We fill in care levels for the homes missing one.
      • The team confirms current noise limits and reminder timing by market.
      • Support confirms the text templates for reminder 1 and reminder 2.
      • Area managers agree how fast a flagged sensor gets fixed.
