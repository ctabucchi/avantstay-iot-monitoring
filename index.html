Monitoring and Code Enforcement

Watch every home like a guard on the block.

We are on call 24/7 for hundreds of homes. A guard on site would listen, look, know who is staying, and act. This plan joins up our sensors, cameras and booking data so a bot does the busywork and people make the calls.

What is this?

A bot that triages every noise alert, so agents only touch the ones that need a person.

Agents keep one job: listen on Ring and decide. The bot does the rest. It gathers the facts, posts one tidy alert, and writes the log. Later, it sends the first guest reminder on clear cases.

  • Listen

    NoiseAware sensors hear noise inside and out. They raise an alert after 5 or more minutes over the limit.

  • Look

    Ring cameras let an agent hear and see the home and next door. Is it a party, wind, or a neighbor?

  • Know

    The booking says who is there, how many, and how risky the stay is. Plus which Ring login and which neighbors to check.

  • Decide

Why care?

Most of the team's time goes to confirming nothing needs doing.

Alerts pile up at night, when guests party and fewer people are on shift. Most turn out to be fine. Every one still takes a person to look up, listen, and log by hand.

      How it connects

      Build on what already works.

      Voyage already gets NoiseAware alerts and matches them to the booking. Its Slack bot, Alex, posts them for a few homes today. Turn that on for every high-risk home, send it to one channel, and let an automation tool like Make.com or Zapier do the rest. No new engineering to start.

      Why not NoiseAware's own auto-reminder?

      We tried it and turned it off. It texted guests every 10 minutes on every alert, including wind and broken sensors. Our version runs our rules first, and a person confirms before any guest hears from us until the bot has earned trust.

      Why not the NoiseAware API?

      It is still an option for later. It gives the noise graph and which sensor fired, which helps spot bad sensors. But Alex already does the hard part, matching alerts to bookings, so we start there and add the API only if we need it.

      The plan

      Four phases. Each one earns the next.

      Nothing talks to a guest on its own until it has matched our agents' calls at least 90% of the time.

      1. Phase 0. No build

        Get the data right

        Turn on Alex alerts for every High and Very High home, into one channel. Fill in each home's Ring account and neighbors. Give a risk tier to the homes missing one.

        Done when: every high-risk home posts to one channel and has a Ring and neighbor entry.

      2. Phase 1

        Triage and log

        The bot reads each alert, adds booking, risk, Ring and neighbors, and opens a thread. The agent taps an outcome. The bot writes the report row.

        Done when: no one polls the dashboard, and 90% of report rows are written by the bot.

      3. Phase 2

        Spot bad sensors

        Flag alerts in empty homes, alerts that lag the graph, and flat lines. Draft the reminder text for the agent.

        Done when: a faulty sensor list is reviewed weekly and each one has a repair owner.

      4. Phase 3

        First reminder, automatic

        On clear cases, the bot sends the first guest reminder. It runs for two weeks in a test channel first, saying what it would send.

        Done when: it agrees with agents on 90% of cases, then goes live.

      The details

      Requirements, in plain English.

      Who it is for
      Monitoring agents on every shift, their lead, and area managers who get escalations.
      Goals
      Agents never poll a dashboard. Every alert arrives with the facts attached. Every incident is logged once, by the bot. Bad sensors get found and fixed.
      Not goals
      Replacing agents. Judging noise from Ring audio. Calling police, the city, or removing guests.
      How we will know it works
      Time from alert to decision. Share of alerts closed with no agent. Share of report rows written by the bot. Reminders sent when Ring was quiet. Number of open faulty sensors.
      The rules it follows
      From our noise SOP. A violation is 5+ minutes over the limit. Stay risk is the higher of home risk and guest risk. Low and Medium get leeway unless neighbors have complained recently. High: check Ring first. Very High: strongest text and a call right away. Wait 10 minutes after a reminder, then escalate.

      Always stays human

      • Listening on Ring and judging the noise
      • Calls with the city or police
      • Second reminders and guest removals
      • Neighbor and owner complaints

      Risks and how we handle them

      • Wrong home data. Some homes share an ID in the source export. We check IDs before the bot uses them.
      • Missing risk tiers. About 1 in 7 homes in NoiseAware has none. The owner of risk tiers fills them in Phase 0.
      • Rules are out of date. The SOP is from 2023. The team confirms thresholds by market before Phase 1.
      • Texting guests by mistake. No automatic texts until shadow mode hits 90% agreement.
      • Shared logins. Passwords move out of documents and get rotated.
